# E1 — Bootstrap vs elder

Spec §10 E1, §5.4, §5.7. Segment G4b. Defaults: q_edge 0.08, θ 0.4, 20 agents, ER contact p = 0.2.

## 1. Plain summary

We took one "experienced" simulated mind, grown the normal way in another population, and dropped it into a group of 19 newcomers. We then compared three sets of rules for how minds can advance. Under the default rule, where only equals can advance together, the experienced mind was useless: nobody could learn from it and the group developed exactly as it would have without it. When newcomers could advance only by learning from someone ahead of them, the group developed more slowly at first, very unevenly, and every newcomer ended up with a copy of the same few patterns. Allowing both kinds of learning worked best by far: faster progress, more minds reaching higher stages, and more varied patterns. But almost all of that gain came from newcomers teaching each other, not from the experienced mind. A side finding matters for every experiment: once a mind advances, it tends to get stuck in a small corner of its own repertoire. This limits how well it can respond to others, and in particular how well an experienced mind can teach.

## 2. Method

**Elder.** For each seed s, a donor bootstrap population (same config, seed 10 000 + s) runs until its best mind reaches level 5. If it hasn't within 1 000 epochs, the donor is rerun with seed + 1 000, up to 5 attempts. That mind is transplanted, with its graph, current node and library intact, as agent 0 of a fresh population built from seed s. 15/16 elders reached level 5. Seed 3's five donors all stalled at level 4, so its elder is level 4. The donor attempts per seed were `[1,1,2,5,2,1,2,1,4,1,3,2,2,1,2,1]`.

**Conditions.** Every condition uses the same 20 fresh minds and contact graph for a given seed. The only difference is agent 0 and the mode. All metrics are over agents 1–19, so the elder is always excluded.

| Condition | mode | agent 0 |
|---|---|---|
| (a) bootstrap+elder | bootstrap | transplanted elder |
| (b) elder+elder | elder | transplanted elder |
| (c) mixed+elder | mixed | transplanted elder |
| reference: bootstrap (no elder) | bootstrap | ordinary fresh mind |
| reference: mixed (no elder) | mixed | ordinary fresh mind |

Elder mode without an elder is omitted, because no level-up is possible in it. Runs are 400 epochs with 16 seeds (0–15). Uncertainty is mean ± standard error across seeds.

**Metrics.**
- Mean level, and the fraction at levels ≥1, ≥2 and ≥3, every epoch.
- The first epoch any non-elder mind reaches levels 1–5.
- The median epoch at which a mind reaches level 1, counting minds that never do as 400.
- Distinct flat words per level (`analysis.metrics.distinct_flat_words`, elder excluded).
- **Distinct new content** per level: each record's extras reduced to its accepted offers as (self/other, label), with the lexicographic minimum taken over the word and its self↔other swap. This ignores the wildcard padding and the perspective flip that transmission adds (§5.7). Two copies of the same source word therefore count once, however many hands they passed through.
- Level-ups split by source (bootstrap or elder) and by partner (agent 0 or another mind).
- The elder's reach: its degree, its connected component, and how many minds it taught directly.

**Commands** (about 17 min wall time on 3 workers, plus 2.5 min for the elder-profile backfill described below):

```
uv run python -m experiments.E1 --config experiments/E1.yaml --seeds 16 --workers 3 --tag E1
uv run python -m experiments.E1_plot --tag E1
```

Raw data: `outputs/E1/E1.json`; tables: `outputs/E1/summary.md`. The elder-profile fields (`learned_labels`, `sync_available_at_x`) were added after the main run. They were backfilled by regrowing each elder from its recorded donor seed, and the donor epochs and level were checked to match. A fresh run of the command above records them directly.

### The elder's reach in pure elder mode

- **Contact reach is not the limit.** The elder had degree 4.0 ± 0.5 (range 1–8) and reached all 19 minds in 14 of 16 seeds (18 of 19 in the other two). It met someone in 84% of epochs.
- **Teaching was very uneven.** The fraction of minds at level ≥1 at epoch 400 varied from 0.05 to 1.00 across seeds.
- **What predicts it is the elder's repertoire.** Every elder had ended up sitting on a node where only the labels it had itself learned were available: 2 labels for 13 elders, 3 for the other 3.

| Elder's learned labels | seeds | frac ≥1 @400 | mean level @400 | first learner L1 (epoch) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 13 | 0.48 ± 0.10 | 2.15 ± 0.47 | 111 ± 35 |
| 3 | 3 | 1.00 ± 0.00 | 4.32 ± 0.21 | 15 ± 12 |

Diagnostics on seeds 5, 10 and 15 over 60 elder-mode epochs looked at contingency: how often the elder answers a newcomer's offer with its complement. The newcomers' mean contingency against the elder was 0.03 (seed 10), 0.15 (seed 15) and 0.44 (seed 5), against θ = 0.4. In 60 epochs, only 12 of 576 learner windows qualified.

## 4. Interpretation against the spec's hypotheses

- **Elder vs bootstrap speed (E1).** "Elder mode is faster" does not hold in general.
  - Pure elder mode starts slowly: first L1 at about epoch 93, against epoch 0 in bootstrap. The first learner must satisfy contingency against an elder who can answer only 2–3 labels.
  - Once a few minds have been taught, transmission cascades through the population. 83% of elder-mode level-ups came from non-elder "teachers".
  - By epoch 400, elder mode passes bootstrap on mean level (2.56 vs 1.41) and on the fraction at level ≥2 (0.57 vs 0.24). It is still rising, and between-seed spread is large.
  - It is capped at the elder's level, and it leaves 42% of minds at level 0.
- **Bootstrap requires equal levels (§13.5).** This is the binding constraint in bootstrap mode. The level ≥2 fraction freezes at about 0.24 by epoch 30 and never moves again. A lone elder cannot help, because nobody shares its level. Condition (a) is indistinguishable from no elder on every metric.
- **Mixed mode dominates.** Mixed mode with no elder at all already more than doubles the mean level (3.48 vs 1.43) and triples the level ≥2 fraction. Adding the elder gives a small extra push at the top: first L4 at 17 vs 33 epochs and first L5 at 54 vs 76. Final means differ by about 1 SE, so the effect is not significant at this n.
- **Structural diversity.** Transmission copies rather than creates. In elder mode, "distinct new content" is exactly 1 per level: every mind carries the elder's words.
  - **Distinct flat words overstate diversity under transmission.** Elder mode shows 5–8 distinct flat words per level. They come from two sources. Each learner keeps its own REF prefix. And each hand-to-hand transmission changes the word: transmitting twice gives `SELF_ANY OTHER_ANY e SELF_ANY OTHER_ANY`, so every generation of the chain yields a new flat word with the same content.
  - Mixed mode has the most real diversity at levels 2–5, well above bootstrap, because more minds get high enough to invent new extras.

## 5. Surprises and caveats

1. **Absorbing grown nodes (§6), the main surprise.** It is a model issue, not specific to E1.
   - Growth activates a node v with one edge anchor → v and only self-loops out of v: a τ self-loop plus self-loops for learned labels. Nothing leads out of v, so once a mind steps into v it stays there forever, with only its learned labels available.
   - In a bootstrap check (seed 3, 200 epochs), **all 16 minds at level ≥1 sat on a grown node with no exit**, with 1–3 sync labels. Five had only 1 label, which makes contingency uncomputable, since it needs 2 labels. Those minds can never level up again.
   - This plausibly explains the bootstrap plateau at level ≥2 ≈ 0.24, the stalling donors (9 of 16 first donor seeds never reached level 5 in 1 000 epochs), and the elders' weak teaching.
   - It likely affects E2–E5 as well.
2. Elder-mode curves had not settled by epoch 400, and seed-to-seed variance is large (SE ≈ 0.44 on the mean level). The final numbers for (b) are lower bounds on the asymptote.
3. Elders are selected by "first mind to reach level 5". That favours minds from fast donor populations, and seed 3's elder is level 4.
4. In the no-elder references, "agent 0" is an ordinary mind. The small "with agent 0" counts in those conditions (`E1_sources.png`) are not elder effects.
5. The distinct-new-content metric is my own construction. It deliberately merges words that differ only by wildcards or τ moves.

## 6. Suggested follow-ups

- **Fix or ablate the absorbing-node effect, then rerun E1 and the calibration.** For example, give the new node v an edge back to its anchor (v → anchor on τ), or copy the anchor's out-edges to v.
- Run elder mode longer (1 000+ epochs) to find its asymptote.
- Vary the elder's level (3, 5, 8) and the number of elders.
- Place the elder at a hub versus a leaf of the contact graph. Reach was not binding here, but with repertoire fixed it might be.
- Use an elder that has been "unstuck" (moved back to an original node) to separate repertoire from level.
- Relate the mixed-mode gain to E5, since transmission among peers is a private-language channel.
