# E1 under spec v0.3 — Bootstrap vs elder, re-run

Spec §10 E1, §5.4, §5.7; segment G4c. The only change from v0.2 is D10: a node created by growth now gets a τ edge back to its anchor (`model/graph.py`, `activate_node`), so developed minds can walk back out of it. Design and scripts are the same as in `results/E1.md`, which stays as the v0.2 baseline. Defaults: q_edge 0.08, θ 0.4, 20 agents, ER contact p = 0.2, 400 epochs.

## 1. Plain summary

Once minds can no longer get stuck in newly grown parts of their own repertoire, an experienced mind becomes a good teacher. When newcomers can only advance by learning from someone ahead of them, the whole group now gets going immediately, and every newcomer learns something, where before it took about 90 epochs and many never learned anything. Letting minds both invent together and learn from those ahead is still the best rule, but only slightly better than learning from the elder alone. Adding the experienced mind to that mixed group makes no difference to the final result. Under the default rule, where only equals can advance together, the experienced mind is still useless. Two v0.2 findings hold unchanged: when newcomers can only learn from the elder, everyone ends up with copies of the same few patterns, and most of the teaching is passed on between newcomers rather than coming from the elder directly.

## 2. What was run

Same design as v0.2 (see `results/E1.md` §2): for each seed s, an elder is grown in a donor bootstrap population (seed 10 000 + s, retried with +1 000 up to 5 times) until its best mind reaches level 5. It is then transplanted as agent 0 into five conditions: bootstrap+elder, elder+elder, mixed+elder, and the no-elder references bootstrap and mixed. All metrics are over the 19 non-elder minds. Uncertainty is mean ± SE across seeds. v0.2 comparisons use the **matched seeds 0–11** unless marked "v0.2 (16 seeds)".

**Judgment calls:**
- **12 seeds (0–11) instead of 16.** Seeds cost 2–8 min each under v0.3 on a shared machine, and the 30-minute budget with 2 workers covers 12. The first 12 seeds are identical to v0.2's, so every comparison is paired.
- **Elder level kept at 5.** It is still "high" under v0.3. Without an elder, the best non-elder bootstrap mind reached only 5.4 ± 0.6 by epoch 400, and bootstrap first reached L5 in just 6 of 12 seeds. Donors needed 28–900 epochs to produce a level-5 mind. Donors stall less, though: 10 of 12 succeeded on the first attempt (v0.2: 5 of 12 on these seeds), and seed 3 now gets a level-5 elder where v0.2 had to settle for level 4.

**Commands** (28.5 min wall time on 2 workers; the machine was shared with four other runs):

```
uv run python -m experiments.E1 --config experiments/E1.yaml --seeds 12 --workers 2 --tag E1_v03
uv run python -m experiments.E1_plot --tag E1_v03 --prefix E1_v03
uv run python -m experiments.E1_compare --old <v0.2 outputs>/E1/E1.json --new outputs/E1_v03/E1.json
```

The v0.2 raw data is `outputs/E1/E1.json`, produced by the G4b run at tag `v0.2-results`. `E1_plot.py` gained a `--prefix` option, whose default `E1` keeps its old behaviour. `E1_compare.py` is new; it plots the paired v0.2/v0.3 curves and prints the matched-seed table. Raw v0.3 data: `outputs/E1_v03/E1.json`; full tables: `outputs/E1_v03/summary.md` (reproduced below).

Figures: `E1_v03_development.png`, `E1_v03_diversity.png`, `E1_v03_sources.png` (same layout as v0.2), and `E1_v03_compare.png` (v0.2 dashed vs v0.3 solid, per condition).

- **Elder mode is now fast.** The first learner reaches L1 at epoch 0.6 ± 0.3 (v0.2 on these seeds: 78 ± 31). The median mind reaches L1 at epoch 6.4 ± 0.4 (v0.2, 16 seeds: 249 ± 41). All 19 minds are at level ≥2 by about epoch 25 in every seed.
- **Elder mode plateaus below the elder.** Its mean level is 3.85 at epoch 300 and 3.96 at 400, and only 23 ± 6% of learners reach L5, the elder's level, by epoch 400.
- **Bootstrap still freezes.** In both bootstrap conditions, 16% of minds never reach L1 (v0.2: 15–16%), and the level ≥2 fraction stops at about 0.74 by epoch 100. The level cap is higher than in v0.2, but the equal-level lock-in is the same.

### The elder

- **The elder is no longer confined to its learned labels.** The number of sync labels available at its current node is 2–6, against 2–3 in v0.2, where it always equalled the elder's learned labels. Several elders have more labels available than they learned; seed 10's elder, for example, learned 2 and has 6 available. That means they have walked back out of their grown nodes.
- **Repertoire no longer predicts reach.** Every elder-mode population ends with all reachable minds at level ≥1 (0.95–1.00, where 1.00 counts only minds the elder can reach). Mean final level is 4.25 ± 0.22 for 2-label elders (n = 4), 4.04 ± 0.11 for 3 labels (n = 4), 3.63 ± 0.18 for 4 labels (n = 3) and 3.42 for 5 labels (n = 1). This is a weak inverse trend at small n; v0.2's positive dependence is gone.
- Contact reach is unchanged, because the graphs are the same as in v0.2: degree 1–7, and the elder meets someone in 84% of epochs.

| v0.2 conclusion | v0.3 | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| "Elder mode starts slowly: first L1 at about epoch 93, since learners must satisfy contingency against a 2–3-label elder" | First L1 at epoch 0.6; +1.14 ± 0.12 levels over bootstrap by epoch 25 (v0.2: −0.86 ± 0.21) | **Reversed.** The slow start came from the trapped elder |
| Elder mode leaves many minds at level 0, and teaching is very uneven across seeds (frac ≥1 from 0.05 to 1.00) | frac ≥1 = 1.00 in every seed; mean-level SE 0.12 vs 0.48 | **Reversed** |
| "The elder's repertoire (2 vs 3 labels) decides its reach" | No positive dependence; if anything, a weak inverse trend | **Reversed / obsolete**; it was a trap artefact |
| Elder mode eventually passes bootstrap | Passes it from the start, by 1.6 levels at epoch 400 | **Survives, stronger** |
| Elder mode is capped at the elder's level | Plateaus at a mean level of about 4; 23% of learners reach L5 | **Survives** |
| Bootstrap requires equal levels: a lone elder can't help, and (a) ≈ no elder | (a) − bootstrap = −0.22 ± 0.15; the elder is never a bootstrap partner | **Survives** (point estimate slightly negative in both versions) |

## 5. Caveats

1. **n = 12 seeds, not 16.** SEs are about 15% wider than a 16-seed run would give. The paired design offsets part of this. The contrasts that decide the verdicts are all well outside 2 SE; the exceptions are elder-vs-mixed at epoch 400 (1.5 SE) and elder-in-bootstrap (1.5 SE), which I report as "no clear difference".
2. The v0.2 numbers in the contrast tables are recomputed on seeds 0–11 from the v0.2 raw data. They differ slightly from the 16-seed numbers in `results/E1.md`; for example, elder mode at epoch 400 is 3.03 on these seeds against 2.56 on all 16.
3. Elder mode's plateau at a mean of about 4, below the elder's level 5, is unexplained. Candidates: learners can't qualify their top record against the elder once its contingency is saturated, or they learn from lower-level peers who can't carry them to L5. It was not diagnosed.
4. The 16% of bootstrap minds that never reach L1 is the same in v0.2 and v0.3, so it is not a trap effect. The most likely cause is stranding: a mind whose neighbours have all advanced has no equal-level partner. This fits E3's stranding finding, but was not verified here.
5. The elder is still chosen as "first mind to reach level 5", which favours fast donors. In mixed mode an elder that is L5 matters little, because non-elder minds reach L5 by themselves in 9 of 12 seeds.
6. The distinct-new-content metric is my own construction; see the caveat in `results/E1.md`.

## 6. Suggested follow-ups

- Diagnose the elder-mode plateau below the elder's level: track per-learner windows against the elder after L3.
- Measure the elder's effect on *speed* in mixed mode with more seeds. The first-L5 difference is the only place it might matter.
- Vary the elder's level (3, 5, 8). Under v0.3 a higher elder should lift elder mode's ceiling roughly one-for-one, if the cap is the only limit.
- Test stranding as the cause of bootstrap's L0 residue, by checking whether all of an L0 mind's neighbours are at level ≥1 at the end.
